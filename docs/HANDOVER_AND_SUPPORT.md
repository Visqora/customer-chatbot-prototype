# Handover and Support

## 1. Handover package

The freelancer delivers:

- Client-owned chatbot workspace and production bot access.
- Final source inventory and knowledge-base configuration.
- Recorded platform plan, limits, and recurring-cost responsibility.
- Accepted bot instructions and widget settings, exported where the platform supports it.
- Website installation location and rollback procedure.
- Final acceptance report and open-issue record.
- Knowledge-update guide and deployment runbook.
- Access-removal and data-deletion confirmation.
- One owner-training session of up to 45 minutes.

Provider exports may not capture every managed-platform setting. Any non-exportable configuration should be recorded with non-sensitive screenshots or a settings checklist.

## 2. Training agenda

The client owner will practice:

1. Signing in with client-owned access and multi-factor authentication.
2. Finding the active bot, knowledge sources, usage, and conversation logs.
3. Adding or replacing an approved source.
4. Removing a superseded source and waiting for indexing.
5. Testing direct, paraphrased, edge-case, and fallback questions.
6. Publishing an approved content change.
7. Reviewing unresolved questions without unnecessarily exporting personal data.
8. Checking plan usage and billing.
9. Disabling the website widget or rolling back its installation.
10. Inviting and removing collaborators.

Training must use synthetic scenarios, not real customer records.

## 3. Handover checklist

### Ownership

- [ ] Client controls account email, recovery method, multi-factor authentication, and billing.
- [ ] Client has owner or administrator access to chatbot and website configuration.
- [ ] No production dependency uses the freelancer's personal account or payment method.
- [ ] Client has confirmed ownership of content and business data.

### Operations

- [ ] Client can update, remove, index, test, and publish knowledge.
- [ ] Client can review fallback questions and plan usage.
- [ ] Human-support route and owner are recorded.
- [ ] Rollback steps and responsible person are recorded.
- [ ] Provider support links and account identifiers are recorded without secrets.

### Security and closure

- [ ] Freelancer website access is removed or reduced as agreed.
- [ ] Freelancer chatbot collaborator access is removed or reduced as agreed.
- [ ] Temporary tokens and shared links are revoked.
- [ ] Local credentials, source copies, exports, transcripts, and screenshots are deleted.
- [ ] No real customer data was used in the demo or acceptance evidence.
- [ ] Deletion and access-removal confirmation is sent in writing.

## 4. Included revisions

The fixed-price scope includes two consolidated pre-launch revision rounds. Each round is one client feedback list submitted against a review build.

Included revisions cover:

- Correcting behavior against approved source content.
- Adjusting approved greeting, fallback, tone, and concise wording.
- Replacing or correcting in-scope knowledge sources.
- Adjusting standard widget colors, icon, launcher position, and approved page coverage.
- Correcting acceptance-test failures within the agreed scope.

The following require change approval and may require a new quote:

- New channels, websites, languages, bots, or business units.
- New API, order-status, CRM, helpdesk, authentication, or payment integrations.
- Custom-coded widget behavior beyond standard platform controls.
- More than 20 sources or 50 acceptance questions.
- New policy authoring, substantial content restructuring, or product catalog ingestion.
- Platform migration after the original tool was approved.

## 5. Post-launch defect period

The included defect period lasts seven calendar days from successful production deployment.

A covered defect is reproducible behavior that fails the accepted requirements using the same approved configuration, content, website environment, and platform capabilities. Covered fixes include incorrect configured fallback, broken in-scope links, missed approved widget settings, and regressions caused by the delivered installation.

The defect period does not include:

- New features or changed business policies.
- New or revised source content.
- Client or third-party changes made after acceptance.
- Provider outages, provider feature changes, or usage-limit exhaustion.
- Website/theme/plugin changes unrelated to the delivered installation.
- Ongoing conversation review or knowledge maintenance.

Report a defect with the time, page URL, browser/device, exact synthetic question, observed result, expected result, and non-sensitive evidence. Severity 1 issues should also trigger widget rollback where appropriate.

## 6. Support response targets

During the included defect period, proposed targets are:

| Severity | Acknowledgement target | Action target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Severity 1 | Within 4 business hours | Mitigate or roll back as soon as practical |
| Severity 2 | Within 1 business day | Fix or provide a plan within 2 business days |
| Severity 3-4 | Within 2 business days | Resolve within the defect period when covered |

These are support targets, not a 24/7 service-level agreement. Confirm working hours and contact channel at kickoff.

## 7. Ongoing operation after support

The client is responsible for:

- Keeping policies, products, sizing, and contact details current.
- Reviewing unresolved questions and plan consumption.
- Maintaining platform billing and website compatibility.
- Managing collaborators and retention settings.
- Testing changes before publication.

Ongoing monitoring or maintenance may be purchased separately under a new written scope.
